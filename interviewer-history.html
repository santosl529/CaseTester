What the turn times say

  • Across eight batches and two model families, the median model call never left 1.8–2.0s. Every prompt-and-guard change in between was latency-neutral.
  • Two changes moved it, and both were slower: thinking (B6, +0.8s median, p95 nearly doubled) and the declaration-first JSON schema (B10).
  • Streaming (B9) brought the first spoken segment to 1.75s. That's still above the 1.5s voice budget, and the whole turn got slower (2.37s). The streaming spec expected this: it's a prerequisite for voice, not the fix.
  • B10 is a regression worth checking before that branch merges. The model now writes move and a full requests list before any say text, so nothing can be spoken until the declarations finish. On a request-heavy persona like Nikhil, the first segment went from 1.75s to 3.27s. One run is a small sample, but the mechanism is structural.

Quote and claim accuracy

“Quotes” shows up in two places in this system: the interviewer quoting or paraphrasing the candidate live, and the scoring report citing candidate quotes as evidence. Both have a history.

Are the report's quotes real?

I checked every quote in every final report (wentWell, needsWork, and strong-checklist quotes) against the candidate's own turns, splitting on ellipses the way the evidence audit does. This was an independent local script, not the product's audit.

The 7 “not verbatim” quotes (B1, B2, B4) are near-verbatim: quotes nested inside quotes, or a dropped clause. Patterns across the history

My reading of the record, not measured.

Enforcement moved from the prompt into code

Most September and October fixes are deterministic guards that repair the turn after the model writes it: same-turn resolution, forced and stale releases, close replacement, the synthesis guard, probe withholding. The prompt grew slowly; the runner grew from 148 to 1,205 lines. The model now mostly proposes, and code decides what gets said.

Each model switch changed the failure shape

Haiku missed math. Opus praised mid-case (~9 in B3) and sometimes ignored requests. Sonnet spoke its own reasoning. Each switch was followed by a round of new strips and guards tuned to the new model's phrasing. That's also why the meta-leak patterns had to be rewritten after the Opus → Sonnet switch.

The checks were a recurring source of errors

Several regressions came from the guards rather than the model:

  • The recompute check caused all three false corrections and the $480M leak.
  • The unit check fired on every correct 10.5.
  • The progress detector couldn't read number words.
  • The goodbye detector missed phrasings.
  • Coverage let a case end with no recommendation ask.
  • The narration rule blanked turns.

The spec's own design principle (check the least typical form of a risk) comes from this.

Hand-reading caught what the counters didn't

Batch 5's manual review found the ~16 narration lines, false “recommendation received” matches, and unrecognized goodbyes. None of these were counted by any automated check. Batch 6 confirmed it again.

Latency hasn't moved with model changes

Median turn time stayed at 1.8–1.9s across Opus and Sonnet, and caching didn't change it. The levers that did move it were thinking (worse) and the streaming plan (first sentence at ~1.6s on replay).
